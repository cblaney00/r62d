## What this app is

A top-down 2D tactical shooter in the browser for insideabush and up to 9 friends (10
players max, 5v5), played over a VPN to a machine running the server. He wants it to feel
instantly familiar to people who play Rainbow Six Siege — the same round flow, breaching,
reinforcing, droning, cameras, defuser objective, downed-but-not-out — **but everything
must be original**: our own operators, names, map, art. We declined to copy Siege's
operators/maps/art (it would be recreating a specific existing game); keep it that way.

His wishes so far:
- Seven maps picked in the lobby (Harbor Office, Cliffside Villa, Rail Depot, The Embassy,
  Alpine Lodge, Clocktower School, and Dust Town: he asked for a classic CS-style desert town, `shared/maps/dust.js`, mostly one floor, `FL.SAND` ground with a `sand()` texture). 51 operators (he chose 15 each first, then asked for at least 5 per role: 21 more were added, see "New operators" below), covering R6's role types with our own names and twists.
  Quick Match only (first to 3 rounds, sides swap every round). Defenders choose the site during operator select.
- Out of sight = dull grey "remembered" map; only what YOU look through is live colour (he asked, no more shared team vision): your eyes, the drone/camera feed you watch (yours or a teammate's), the teammate you spectate (`teamViewers` in main.js, plus open-hatch and stairs-preview viewers derived from it). Teammates' pings/spotted marks still show; a dead teammate gets a big red ✖ + name through walls (`deathMarks`). Enemy changes out of sight stay hidden until seen (`st.known` vs `st.map` in main.js).
- Drone sprint (he asked): Shift on a drone, `DRONE_SPRINT` in constants.js (1.6x, 3 s, 3 a round, 6 s wait), server `p.sprints`/`sprintUntil`/`sprintCd` in tickPlayer, `mods.sprint` in physics `stepMove`, mine `ds`/`dsl`/`dsc`, louder `droneLoop`. Drones are `DRONE_R` 2.5, drawn ×0.7, hit radius 4.5. `node tests/dronesprint.js`.
- Controls are CS2-style (players come from CS2): run default, Shift walk, Ctrl crouch (hold), C lie down (toggle; `o.pr`/`PRONE_T` in physics, `map.under` + `UNDER_KINDS`: drones and low eyes pass/see under desks, cars etc., `castSight(..., low)`), E use, Q skill, 3 melee, 4 gadget. Walking/crouching are silent to others. Running spreads shots (server `fire`). Mouse is pointer-locked in matches with sensitivity + ADS multiplier settings.
- Before you click in (or after tabbing out) the mouse is not captured and your aim simply stays put (it used to follow the loose pointer and bounce with the camera, he noticed); a pulsing "CLICK TO TAKE CONTROL" (`#clickIn`) shows, and that first click only takes control (`st.lockAsked`), it doesn't shoot. The camera snaps (no swoop) when its target jumps >420 px, and the sightline look-ahead settles more gently.
- Aiming mode "Rotate" (`spin`, added when he still found aiming bad; like Steel Storm / an FPS): the view turns so you always face up the screen (`st.rot` = -π/2 - padAng; `w2s`/`screenToWorld`, the draw matrix `TA..TD`, `#mapLayer` CSS `matrix()`, fog all use it), mouse X turns, Y sets reach (`turnMove`), movement keys are sent with `k.rot` and turned in physics `stepMove` so W walks up the screen, audio pan follows the view (`setListenerRot`). The camera is not clamped to the floor edge while turned (fog blacks out beyond). Text stays upright: `fillText`/`strokeText` on the game and top canvases are wrapped to undo the drawing's rotation round their spot, and labels above things use `UP()` (screen-up in the world). Without a 2× scope the pad/turn/rotate crosshair reaches 4 m further (he asked).
- Aiming style Face (he asked, he chose: only the character turns, 30°, a choice in Settings): `face` in `AIM_MODES`, `faceMove` (like `turnMove`; while aimed in `padAng` stays within `FACE_ADS` 15° of `st.faceC`, the facing when you aimed in), `moveTurn()` turns the keys (Rotate, Face, sprint + W) for `k.rot` and `vaultDir`; thin arc drawn in `crosshair()`.
- Aiming defaults to **Crosshair** (`free` mode, he found the pad horrible): the crosshair moves 1:1 with the mouse like a pointer but is fixed to your character (`aimOff`, `freeMove` in main.js), so walking or the camera sliding never changes your aim; it stays 46 px+ from you and pulling it through the middle comes out the other side; it stops at the screen edge with no hidden travel. Old profiles on the pad switch once (`aimV: 2`). The older modes stay in Settings → Aiming. Before that the default was the **aim pad** (he asked for it then): a ring on the right of the screen (`pad()` in main.js) acts like a joystick — the mouse is kept inside it and its direction from the centre sets the aim, its distance how far ahead the crosshair sits (`cursor()`), so turning round is a short flick.
